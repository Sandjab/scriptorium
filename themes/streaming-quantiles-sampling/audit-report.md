| claim:23 | fusionnabilite-resumes-de-rang | established | corrected | 0 | 2 | 2 | 3 | soutien✗~ réfutation✗~ | A t-digest merge does not preserve the strong centroid ordering of the inputs — the union of two individually strongly-ordered t-digests is in general only weakly ordered, and Dunning & Ertl's paper (arXiv:1902.04023, 2019) itself states this loss of strict ordering 'makes it difficult to compute rigorous error bounds,' unlike GK/KLL whose merged rank error stays provably bounded. | claim:24 | fusionnabilite-resumes-de-rang | contestable | corrected | 0 | 3 | 3 | 5 | soutien✗~ réfutation✗~ source-primaire✗~ | GK is proven space-optimal among deterministic comparison-based quantile summaries on a single machine (Cormode & Veselý, PODS 2020), but this optimality does not transfer to distributed settings requiring repeated merges: GK achieves only one-way mergeability, and the paper that established that framework (Agarwal et al.) notes GK's known merge algorithm 'does not work in an indefinite merging process, so it is not mergeable by our definition.'
