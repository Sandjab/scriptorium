| claim:12 | modeles-rotationnels-rotate-motifs | established | confirmed | 3 | 0 | 0 | 4 | soutien✓ réfutation✓ ré-audit manuel✓ | RotatE calcule le score d'un triplet en traitant chaque relation comme une rotation élément par élément en espace complexe — en attendant t = h∘r, chaque élément du plongement de relation étant contraint à un module unitaire (\|r_i\| = 1, soit r_i = e^{iθ_{r,i}}), via la distance d_r(h,t) = ‖h∘r − t‖ — et s'entraîne avec une perte d'échantillonnage négatif auto-adversarial qui pondère davantage les négatifs les plus difficiles (score le plus élevé) par un softmax à température α sur le lot de négatifs. |
| claim:13 | modeles-rotationnels-rotate-motifs | contestable | corrected | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | soutien✓ réfutation✗~ source-primaire✗~ | RotatE's own analysis (Table 2 and the 'Connection to TransE' paragraph) shows that among the four canonical relation-pattern types it formalizes (symmetry, antisymmetry, inversion, composition), TransE's only excluded pattern is symmetry — a symmetric relation forces the translation vector toward r=0, collapsing related entities to the same point — while TransE can represent antisymmetric, inverse, and compositional relations. This is a narrower and formally distinct claim from the separate, independently well-documented N-1/1-N/N-N multiplicity critique of TransE (from Wang et al. 2014's TransH paper, which shows that shared heads/tails under one relation get pushed together regardless of symmetry). The two critiques address different formal properties (logical relation pattern vs. entity-relation cardinality) and should not be conflated or presented as one subsuming/overstating the other; RotatE's paper does not discuss or debunk the N-1/1-N/N-N framing at all. |
